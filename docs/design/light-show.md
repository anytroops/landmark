# Lights and visuals that follow the set: market, prior work, and what Landmark would add

Status: research only (2026-11-15), for Sean's backlog. Nothing built, nothing decided. Comes after the reconstructor's milestones and the real-set work.

## The idea

A DJ set's lighting and visuals today are either run by a person (a lighting designer or VJ, which most small venues and most DJs do not have) or by software that reacts to the audio or follows cues scripted in advance. Landmark already tells, from the booth audio alone and within about 12 s, which song is playing, where in the song it is, how fast it is being played, and (D43) how two songs are being blended. The idea is to drive lights and visuals from that: look up each playing song's structure (beats, phrases, builds, drops, energy, sections, key), computed once per song offline, and fire looks and effects on the song's own timeline, ahead of what the audio has yet revealed, in any booth and with no preparation by the DJ.

## What exists

Lighting, DJ and venue tier:

- **SoundSwitch** (inMusic): the most used automatic DJ lighting product. It scripts a light show per track ("Autoscript") from the DJ software's beatgrid and phrase analysis, plays it back in sync as the track plays, and has an "Autoloops" mode for unprepared tracks. It needs integration with the DJ software (Serato, VirtualDJ, Engine OS, Traktor, Ableton Link, and since 24 September 2026 rekordbox 7.2.19 and PRO DJ LINK Bridge, with a rekordbox Creative or Professional subscription plus a SoundSwitch license) and a DMX interface (its own Micro or Control One). Reported weaknesses: it depends on the beatgrid (locked at first edit; half-time genres look wrong), it needs the library prepared, and it runs on the DJ's own laptop and software, so it is the DJ's system, not the venue's. [Digital DJ Tips on Autoscript](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/2017/11/soundswitch-update-now-lets-create-light-shows/), [Engine DJ forum](https://community.enginedj.com/t/what-bothers-me-most-about-soundswitch/67266), [rekordbox integration](https://rekordbox.com/en/2026/09/rekordbox-for-mac-windows-and-pro-dj-link-bridge-support-soundswitch/), [The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2026/09/24/rekordbox-pro-dj-link-bridge-lighting-software), [Learn Stage Lighting](https://www.learnstagelighting.com/blog/soundswitch-is-it-a-good-controller-for-dj-lighting%2F).
- **rekordbox Lighting mode** (AlphaTheta, 2018): auto-generated sequences from rekordbox's phrase analysis, through the RB-DMX1 interface (US$349 to 399). Same shape as SoundSwitch: the DJ's software and library. [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/pioneer-dj-add-lighting-mode-rekordbox-watch), [Digital DJ Tips review](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/rbdmx1/).
- **ShowKontrol** (TC Supply with Pioneer DJ): sends PRO DJ LINK track data (which track, position, BPM) from CDJs to lighting and video systems, for shows with an operator. [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/2017/10/pioneer-dj-launches-soundswitch-automated-lighting-competitor/).
- **MaestroDMX** (Limbic Media): a standalone box, about US$850 to €990, that listens to the audio in real time and runs the lights with no preparation, pitched at venues, mobile DJs, bands, and churches. The closest thing to the idea's "any booth" half. Its only independent review (2022) found the "AI" vague; a 2026 trade listing says it analyzes songs in real time with no pre-analysis. [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/maestrodmx-is-an-ai-powered-lighting-designer-in-a-box/), [PLASA listing](https://www.plasashow.com/exhibitor-products/maestrodmx-), [Kirstein pricing](https://www.kirstein.de/en/Live-Studio/Light/Lighting-Controllers/MaestroDMX-Intelligent-Lighting-Control.html), [KPO DJ](https://kpodj.com/maestrodmx-controller--autonomous-dmx-light-shows--standalone-audio-reactive-hardware-p-116296).
- **Lightkey** (Mac), **Lightjams**, and the professional desks (MA, Avolites, ChamSys): tools for a person, with beat sync (MIDI clock, Ableton Link) or timecode. [Lightkey](https://www.lightkeyapp.com), [Learn Stage Lighting on Lightkey](https://learnstagelighting.com/blog/lightkey-is-it-the-right-lighting-software-for-me-interface-guide%2F).
- **Yamaha, "Music:AI for Lighting"** (24 April 2026): real-time analysis of a song's structure and mood driving ambient light, for car interiors, to be sold to automakers. A sign that large audio companies see music-to-light as a product, in a different market. [Yamaha (Japanese)](https://www.yamaha.com/ja/news_release/2026/26042402/), [Driving Vision News](https://www.drivingvisionnews.com/news/2026/05/28/yamahas-immersive-in-car-space-music-light-come-together/).

Visuals (VJ) tier:

- **Resolume** (Avenue, Arena, Wire), **VDMX**, **MadMapper**, **Notch**, **TouchDesigner**: the tools VJs run beside the DJ. Resolume plays and effects pre-rendered clips; TouchDesigner generates visuals live but each show is a development project. [Resolume Wire](https://resolume.com/software/wire), [Resolume vs TouchDesigner](https://immerground.com/blog/resolume-vs-touchdesigner).
- **Real-time AI image generation** in TouchDesigner (StreamDiffusionTD): live diffusion driven by audio or video, on an NVIDIA GPU with TensorRT; its cloud option closes in November 2026. [DotSimulate docs](https://dotsimulate.com/docs/streamdiffusiontd), [Derivative tag](https://derivative.ca/tags/streamdiffusion).
- **Freebeat** (Random Motion Technology, Stanford founders): browser-based AI visuals for DJs and creators, claiming reactive visuals at 3 to 5 s of latency, over a million creators, a Yamaha Creator Pass partnership (March 2026), investment from Yamaha Music Innovations, and a US$6.1M seed round (PitchBook, April 2026). The one funded startup in this space the search found; vendor claims, not independently checked. [Freebeat for DJs](https://freebeat.ai/for-djs), [Rolling Stone UK](https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/culture/how-freebeat-ai-made-music-videos-live-61878/), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/1380955-33), [Yamaha Creator Pass](https://freebeat.ai/articles/freebeat-is-now-part-of-yamaha-creator-pass).

Research:

- **Skip-BART** (Zhao, Jin, Zhou, Zhang; arXiv 2506.01482, revised December 2025): frames automatic stage lighting as generation, not classification; a BART-style model from audio to light hue and intensity per frame, trained on a self-collected dataset of real lighting engineers' work (size not stated in the abstract), with code, data, and weights on GitHub. Claims to beat rule-based methods on every metric and to show "only a limited gap" to real engineers, with no numbers in the abstract. Its stated motivation is the problem with everything above: existing systems "only classify music into limited categories and map them to predefined light patterns," giving "formulaic and monotonous outcomes." [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01482).
- **"Glow with the Flow"** (February 2026, IEEE PacificVis 2026): AI-assisted ambient lightscapes for music videos. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08838).

Market size: no DJ-specific figure exists. DMX lighting control software is estimated at about US$0.95 to 1.13 billion a year (2024 to 2025) growing 6 to 8% a year; programmable stage lighting at about US$2 to 2.4 billion. The incumbents are hardware companies with distribution (inMusic, AlphaTheta, Chauvet). [Intel Market Research](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/dmx-lighting-control-software-2025-2032-210-5549), [Wise Guy Reports](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/dmx-lighting-control-software-market), [Persistence](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/programmable-stage-lighting-market.asp).

## Why what exists disappoints

- **Reactive systems lag.** Audio-reactive lighting (MaestroDMX, every "sound-activated" fixture) can only answer what has already been heard: the strobe fires after the drop starts, the color changes after the section changes. Designers hit the drop on the drop, because they know it is coming.
- **Mapping is shallow.** Bass level to brightness gives the "pumping" look; fixtures act independently with no coordinated design; a mapping that works for house fails for trap. [LED Professional](https://led-professional.com/resources-1/articles/interactive-lighting-control-is-opening-new-doors-for-led-applications), [arXiv 2506.01482](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01482v2).
- **Scripted systems need preparation and integration.** SoundSwitch and rekordbox Lighting mode know the song and its position only because the DJ's software tells them, so they need the DJ's library prepared and the DJ on supported software; a guest DJ with a different laptop, CDJs with a USB stick, or vinyl breaks them. Beatgrid dependence adds errors.
- **The output is a solved problem, the design is not.** DMX, Art-Net, and sACN are standard; fixture libraries exist (AtlaBase, 13,000 devices). What is missing is deciding what looks good when, which is why the academic framing moved from rules to generation.

## What Landmark would add

The missing piece in all of the above is knowing, from the audio alone, what song is playing and where in it the set is. Landmark gives that: song, position, playback rate, and the blend between two songs, from the booth feed, in any booth, with no DJ-side setup. From it:

- **Anticipation without integration.** With a song identified and its position known, its precomputed structure (beats, phrases, builds, drops, sections, energy, key, stems if wanted) becomes a cue track in the song's own time, played back at the detected rate. The system can hit the drop on the drop, like a designer and like SoundSwitch, but like MaestroDMX it needs nothing from the DJ. Nothing in the search does this.
- **Blend-aware transitions.** D43's gain curves say which song is coming in and how fast; looks can crossfade with the music rather than snap at the decoder's song change.
- **Live and offline alike.** The same cue track scores a recorded set for a video edit (the reconstructor's session, with a lighting and visuals timeline), and runs live from the monitor.
- **Falls back when it must.** A song outside the catalog, or the first 12 s of a song (the detection delay, D38), gets reactive lighting: the product is never worse than MaestroDMX.

What it would not solve: the aesthetic design (what a "look" is for this rig and this room), which still needs a model of the venue's fixtures and a vocabulary of looks, and judgment that the research says models approach but have not matched; catalog coverage, which for a venue means a licensed or venue-owned library to analyze; and the top of the market, where a designer runs the show and would use this as an assistant at most.

## Market, honestly

- Venues without an operator, mobile DJs, and small clubs are MaestroDMX's market, at about US$1,000 a unit, and a real but modest one; the software market around it is hundreds of millions a year in total, held by hardware companies with distribution.
- DJs who own their lights are SoundSwitch's market, and SoundSwitch now ships inside rekordbox. Competing there head-on is not attractive.
- Visuals are where the funded startup (Freebeat) is, and where generative AI makes the output itself new; latency of 3 to 5 s is the weakness anticipation would fix.
- The likeliest business shapes: (a) a venue box or software that works off the booth feed, sold on "no prep, any DJ, hits the drop"; (b) the identification-and-anticipation engine licensed to a lighting or visuals company (SoundSwitch's parent inMusic, Chauvet, Freebeat); (c) a feature of the reconstructor (a lighting and visuals timeline for recorded sets). None is venture-scale on its own; (b) has the best fit with the rights-side business already noted in docs/design/set-reconstructor.md, since both sell the same engine.

## If it is built: the first version

1. A cue track per song from offline analysis (beats and downbeats, sections and drops, energy), stored beside the fingerprints; a learned part (Skip-BART's data and code are public) for the look sequence, measured against rule-based looks on their dataset: this is a second ML milestone.
2. The live monitor's song, position, and rate feeding a cue player that emits DMX over Art-Net or sACN, first into a virtual rig (a browser render of a few fixtures), hardware after.
3. Measured before any demo: cue timing error against structure labels at real time (anticipation means the strobe lands within one frame of the drop; the detection delay bounds the first 12 s of each song); song coverage of the catalog on Sean's sets; the share of a set under anticipated versus reactive control.
4. Visuals the same way, with Resolume or TouchDesigner as the renderer and Landmark as the clock and cue source, before any generative model.

Open questions for Sean: whether to aim at venues (a box), DJs (software), or licensing; whether this comes before or after the reconstructor's editor and ML milestones; and whether the lighting rig for a demo exists (a few DMX fixtures and a US$30 to 100 USB interface, or a software rig only).
